Project Design

On this page

    Chassis Organism

    We aim to employ engineered bacteria as a platform for drug production and delivery, and through rational design, make treatment for SCLC more intelligent and personalized. Accordingly, we propose the following requirements for the chassis organism:

    Safety

    The engineered bacteria should adapt to the pulmonary environment, colonize without causing tissue damage, pulmonary inflammation, or excessive immune responses. They must colonize only at target sites and not in healthy tissues. They must not disseminate into the bloodstream, and be unable to survive in external environments. The death of engineered bacteria should be controllable.

    Stability

    The engineered bacteria should stably colonize target sites with acceptable biocompatibility to support long-term administration.

    Targeting

    The engineered bacteria should be able to recognize the tumor microenvironment in the patient's lung, accumulate and colonize at the target site, and initiate drug secretion.

    Secretion

    The engineered bacteria should efficiently synthesize and secrete the designed peptides for treatment.

    Genetic tractability

    The engineered bacteria should be amenable for modular design and experimental validation, facilitating iterative engineering cycles.

    Considering the therapeutic needs of SCLC — compatibility with the pulmonary environment and action at lung tumor sites — we initially selected Lactobacillus plantarum (referencing a previous iGEM project on lung-colonizing engineered bacteria) and Escherichia coli Nissle 1917 (EcN) as core candidate chassis organisms.

    Although Lactobacillus plantarum has precedent in pulmonary delivery from prior projects and exhibits advantages in immunomodulation, our project requires more functional modules and relatively complex drug secretion pathways. Compared with Escherichia coli, lactic acid bacteria have fewer genetic modification tools, lower transformation efficiency, and less sophisticated protein secretion systems than Gram-negative bacteria, which limit the efficiency of peptide synthesis and secretion. Therefore, based on project requirements, we ultimately selected EcN as the chassis for engineering.

    Relative to the aforementioned requirements, EcN presents the following advantages:

    Safe: As a probiotic, EcN has a long history of safe clinical use and is sensitive to multiple antibiotics. As a Gram-negative bacterium, its outer membrane vesicles (OMVs) can induce immune activation without triggering excessive inflammation.

    Stable: EcN is natively facultatively anaerobic and can adapt to the hypoxic pulmonary environment. Although wild-type EcN is not naturally found in the lung, its pulmonary mucosal adhesion can be further improved. Given its high editability, EcN can be engineered to better suit the needs of lung colonization.

    Precise: EcN natively possesses tumor tropism, driven by hypoxia-directed accumulation and adaptation to the immunosuppressive microenvironment. The hypoxic core of tumors attracts facultative anaerobes, while the immunosuppressive tumor milieu permits bacterial survival; in contrast, bacteria are cleared by immune mechanisms in healthy tissues.[1]

    Effective: EcN possesses a well-characterized protein secretion system, making it suitable for peptide production.

    Easy to work with: EcN is supported by a comprehensive toolkit for synthetic biology.

    1. Targeted Pulmonary Delivery via Aerosolization

    Nebulization is employed as the primary administration route to deliver engineered bacteria directly to the lung. By fine-tuning the aerodynamic diameter of aerosol particles, we ensure efficient deposition within the bronchial and distal airway regions — the primary sites of SCLC. Beyond physical delivery, the engineered strain leverages its innate tropism for the unique tumor microenvironment (TME), characterized by hypoxia and high lactate levels. These metabolic cues serve as natural chemoattractants, guiding the bacteria to preferentially colonize the anaerobic, necrotic core of SCLC lesions.

    To further enhance precision, these bacteria are functionalized with surface-expressed DLL3 nanobodies, enabling active molecular recognition of SCLC cells. This multi-stage targeting strategy — integrating optimized physical deposition, innate metabolic tropism, and ligand-receptor mediated sequestration — maximizes intrapulmonary bacterial concentration while strictly minimizing systemic exposure.

    2. Pulmonary Colonization Module: Bronchial Adhesion via P30

    To enable prolonged retention in the respiratory tract, the engineered bacteria heterologously express the Mycoplasma pneumoniae P30 adhesin on their surface. This adhesion module allows the bacteria to anchor to the bronchial epithelium, effectively resisting mucociliary clearance and establishing stable colonization at the tumor-proximal site.

    3. Density-Dependent Activation & Homeostatic Control (Quorum Sensing Circuit)

    A quorum sensing (QS)-based regulatory module is incorporated to ensure population-level control. When bacterial density reaches a predefined threshold, QS signaling activates downstream therapeutic pathways while simultaneously engaging a negative feedback loop to limit further proliferation. This dual-function design maintains population homeostasis and prevents excessive immune stimulation or bacterial overgrowth. Notably, the quorum sensing module is directly linked to therapeutic short peptides, which are secreted through the hemolysin secretion system upon QS activation, so as to solve the problem of short peptide retention.

    4. Metabolic Microenvironment Sensing Module (Lactate-Responsive Circuit)

    A lactate-sensing module is integrated to act as a survival switch for engineered bacteria. Given the elevated lactate levels characteristic of SCLC due to the Warburg effect, this module serves as a key switch to determine the survival of engineered bacteria: the bacteria can only survive and colonize in the lactate-rich tumor microenvironment, while they fail to survive in normal tissues with low lactate levels. This design ensures tumor-specific colonization of the engineered bacteria, laying a foundation for subsequent QS-mediated short peptide secretion.

    5. Therapeutic Effector Module: Hemolysin-Mediated Secretion & Amyloid-Induced Cytotoxicity

    Functional Peptide Design

    First, we analyze the aggregation-prone regions (APRs) of the target protein (such as N-MYC), primarily relying on the TANGO software for analysis. TANGO is a software tool for scoring and predicting protein APRs; it analyzes and scores each amino acid of the input protein sequence one by one, and when the scores of several consecutive amino acids are higher than a specific value, it indicates that this segment is the APR of the protein. After TANGO predicts the APR of the target protein, we slide with a step size of 1 amino acid to find the most suitable window of 5-7 amino acids and design a functional peptide segment (FuncPept) based on the figure below; at the same time, we also combine software such as WALZE to verify and screen our designed FuncPept to ensure it has good aggregation propensity and function. However, the steps of using TANGO, WALZE, and AlphaFold are too cumbersome; to better integrate the functions of these software tools and visualize the results, we are designing a software program to assist our experiments and allow our ideas to be applied as a foundational tool in more innovative fields (for details, please refer to our software section).

    Logic Gates Designed for Tumors

    To ensure that FuncPept only functions in tumor cells without affecting normal cells, we identified Legumain, a protease with high specific expression in SCLC cells. Legumain, also known as Asparaginyl endopeptidase (AEP), is a widely distributed cysteine protease in mammals. The most prominent feature of Legumain is its extremely high recognition specificity for substrate sequences. It almost exclusively cleaves at the carboxyl terminus of asparagine residues. As a typical lysosomal protease, Legumain has the highest catalytic activity in an acidic environment (pH 4.0-6.0). In neutral or alkaline environments, its activity significantly decreases or even undergoes irreversible inactivation.

    We added negatively charged shielding sequences to both ends of the designed FuncPept, which hinder contact between FuncPept and the APR of the target protein, thereby shielding its function. The amino acids connecting FuncPept and the shielding sequence are cleavage sites for Legumain. After our peptide segment enters cancer cells, Legumain cleaves off the negatively charged shielding sequence, thereby enabling FuncPept to exert its function.

    Amyloid Clearance

    The amyloid aggregation caused by long-term retention of FuncPept in the body may induce toxicity. To reduce and degrade these harmful amyloids, we turned our attention to macrophages. Macrophages phagocytize and degrade formed amyloids, thereby reducing their toxic effects.

    To enhance recognition of amyloids by macrophages, we added the macrophage recognition sequence Tuftsin to one side of FuncPept. Tuftsin naturally originates from the Fc segment of the human immunoglobulin IgG heavy chain and is a natural tetrapeptide TKPR released by specific enzymatic cleavage in the spleen; it mainly exerts immunomodulatory effects by binding to specific receptors (such as Neuropilin-1, NRP1) on the surface of macrophages, monocytes, and neutrophils. TKPR is an extremely short sequence that generates minimal steric hindrance and hardly interferes with the core peptide segment in forming a cross-beta-sheet structure. The lysine (K) and arginine (R) in the sequence carry positive charges, making it highly hydrophilic. This ensures that during fiber assembly, the Tuftsin sequence tends to be exposed to the external aqueous environment rather than buried in the hydrophobic fiber core.

    When connecting Tuftsin and FuncPept, we use flexible linker peptide glycine-serine (1-2 GGGGS) repeat sequences; glycine provides flexibility, and serine provides hydrogen bonds to interact with water molecules, ensuring that TKPR is fully exposed outside the fiber skeleton for recognition by membrane receptors on macrophages.

    Combination Therapy

    To address these issues, a combinatorial strategy using INCB3344 and interferon-gamma (IFN-gamma), combined with optimized administration and nanodelivery systems, is proposed. INCB3344, a CCR2 antagonist, blocks the CCL2/CCR2 axis to reduce infiltration of immunosuppressive M2-type TAMs. IFN-gamma drives macrophage polarization toward the pro-inflammatory, anti-tumor M1 phenotype, which enhances phagocytosis, antigen presentation, and anti-tumor immune responses, thereby reversing immunosuppression. Precise dosing regimens, including oral delivery for INCB3344 and injectable or inhalable delivery for IFN-gamma, are recommended, with sequential or simultaneous administration to balance efficacy and safety.

    To counteract these hurdles, we propose a combinatorial strategy centered on Interferon-γ (IFN- γ), integrated with optimized administration protocols and advanced nanodelivery systems. IFN- γ serves as a potent effector to reprogram macrophages toward the pro-inflammatory M1 phenotype, thereby enhancing phagocytosis, antigen presentation, and robust anti-tumor immunity. To balance therapeutic efficacy with safety, we emphasize precise dosing regimens, including inhalable IFN-γ delivery via sequential or simultaneous administration. Furthermore, encapsulating IFN-γ within functional nanocarriers facilitates targeted pulmonary delivery and stimuli-responsive release, maximizing local drug concentration while mitigating systemic toxicity. This integrated approach aims to attenuate pathological inflammation, reshape the TME, and ultimately potentiate anti-tumor responses in SCLC.

    6. Biosafety Module: Programmed Suicide Switch

    To ensure strict biosafety and environmental containment, the ∆msbB - based engineered bacteria are further modified by deleting the endogenous thyA gene, creating a thymidylate synthase-deficient auxotroph. In vitro, the ParaC promoter drives the expression of the thyA gene in the presence of arabinose, effectively complementing the thymidine auxotrophy and allowing for robust bacterial expansion; Upon administration, the control of thyA shifts to the lactate-responsive promoter PlldR. This transition creates a strict survival dependency on the high-lactate milieu characteristic of the SCLC TME.
