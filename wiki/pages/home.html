THE PROBLEM

Both in China and worldwide, the incidence of cancer is on the rise, threatening human health and claiming lives at an alarming rate.

China's lifetime cancer incidence risk is about 27.6%.
The global lifetime cancer incidence risk is about 25.1%, meaning that roughly one in four people will face a cancer threat in their lifetime.

China's point prevalence rate is about 0.34%, while the global point prevalence rate is about 0.67%, highlighting the urgent demand in cancer treatment.

Among all cancers, lung cancer remains the leading cause of cancer death. As a subtype of lung cancer, small cell lung cancer (SCLC) has become one of the most difficult types of cancer to treat due to its high malignancy, rapid progression, and early metastasis.

Why SCLC?

SCLC NSCLC

Compared with NSCLC, SCLC has almost no effective targeted therapies and few reliable biomarkers, making it a top priority for innovative therapeutic strategies.

Current treatments for SCLC have obvious limitations:

Second-line therapies, including small-molecule targeted drugs and bispecific T-cell engagers, also have limited efficacy.

The mainstream treatment combines chemotherapy with immunotherapy, which has a certain initial tumor shrinkage effect but unsustainable results, accompanied by prominent drug resistance and high recurrence rate.

Additionally, SCLC is hard to screen early due to its aggressive progression and early metastasis; limited clinical biomarkers further trap its treatment in a dilemma of "difficult diagnosis, difficult cure, high recurrence rate, and high cost".
